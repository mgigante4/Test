DFT, IDFT, Frequency Bins, and Aliasing

N-point discrete Fourier transform

$$X[k]=\sum_{n=0}^{N-1}x[n]e^{-j2\pi kn/N}$$

$$0\le k<N$$

N-point inverse DFT

$$x[n]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k=0}^{N-1}X[k]e^{j2\pi kn/N}$$

$$0\le n<N$$

DFT samples the DTFT

$$X[k]=X(e^{j\omega_k})$$

$$\omega_k=\frac{2\pi k}{N}$$

Frequency bin in hertz

$$f_k=\frac{kF_s}{N}$$

Frequency spacing

$$\Delta f=\frac{F_s}{N}$$

Sampling time

$$T_s=\frac{1}{F_s}$$

Sampling angular frequency

$$\omega=\frac{2\pi f}{F_s}$$

Nyquist frequency

$$f_{\text{Ny}}=\frac{F_s}{2}$$

Avoid aliasing of bandlimited signals when $F_s>2f_{\max}$ (with appropriate bandlimit assumptions).

DFT periodicity

$$X[k+N]=X[k]$$

Bins above N/2 represent negative frequencies:

$$f_k=\frac{(k-N)F_s}{N}$$

Use this mapping for $k>N/2$.

Frequency-domain undersampling

Keep $M$ equally spaced bins of an N-point DFT with $N=LM$:

$$Y[r]=X[Lr]$$

$$0\le r<M$$

M-point IDFT recovers aliased samples:

$$y[n]=\sum_{q=0}^{L-1}x[n+qM]$$

$$0\le n<M$$

Example: N=8, M=4, L=2.

$$Y[r]=X[2r]$$

$$y[n]=x[n]+x[n+4]$$

For $x[n]=[1,2,4,8,16,32,64,128]$:

$$y[n]=[17,34,68,136]$$

This result assumes the bins $X[0],X[2],X[4],X[6]$ are kept. Choosing another set of four bins can give a different answer.
